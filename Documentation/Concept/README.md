# Concept Documentation
Design Report Concept and Testing plan - paper prototype


Design Report Concept

Selected App & Rationale
In this XR project, I choose Microsoft Word as the base, and make a simulation of a text editing application with a “creator space” idea. In this space, creators can focus on their content in a free and immersive way to improve efficiency and express their thoughts.
The reason I choose this topic is: First, text processing is a high-frequency task in daily study and creation. The immersive space and more direct file display can help improve working efficiency and thinking fluency. Second, traditional text editing software is not very direct to support personalised expression and recording inspiration. XR can try to use immersive features and stylised interaction to help users record their ideas better. Also, I hope to use the XR spatial layout and interaction system to rebuild the atmosphere of creation and give users a more emotional space to support inspiration.
User Tasks & Goals
This project is aimed at students and creators who have needs for writing, idea recording, and content creation. The goals of this group include: Quickly and timely record inspiration content, like using sticky notes or creating idea record files; Organize and archive the created content, put virtual files into cloud space or virtual folders; Immersive and supportive creation space
So, in the idea, the main tasks of the user will include: putting sticky notes and typing content in them, typing content in virtual papers, and putting the content into folders (virtual bookshelf). These interactions with the environment and the strong visual content they create will improve the experience of using writing software.
Concept development Process
In the idea development process, I first started from copying the software function and had conceptual discussion with classmates. During the communication, I gradually turned to a more XR-based space expression, and tried to make low-fidelity paper model to test the logic. At the same time, through classroom user testing and interviews, I realized that creation should not only focus on editing text or building documents in the software, but also include organizing inspiration and space emotion. Based on this, I started to sketch the space design by observing real world life habits and user’s physical actions, and tried to add elements like sticky notes, archive drawer and virtual papers. I want to transform the text tool into a creator’s space.Also,based on the feedback in prototype testing, I made some updates to improve the design. First, I changed the original “drag and drop to store” into an automatic adsorption system that helps the document directly stick to the archive wall, to reduce the problem of too many stacked papers and unclear view. And also changed the text prompts in the space (like “drag, type, and stick on wall”) into more clear and guided ones, such as “drag a sticker and type your inspiration then put it on the wall” to reduce misunderstandings. In the end, I learned a lot from the feedback about making the interaction flow simpler. I removed some unneeded clicks to make the whole experience smoother.

XR Concept Design
The core of this XR concept design is to create a calm and friendly creator space to support users’ free creative behaviour. In the current writing process, users often face problems like having ideas but not enough time to write them down, or having too many drafts that are hard to organise. This also means that the overall creative efficiency and idea development is not strong enough.
Therefore, in this space, users can freely pull out sticky notes from the desk to record short ideas and drafts, type on the virtual paper, and drag the paper into the file cabinet after finishing. It helps to reduce the problems of unclear layers, mixed drafts, and missing detail information in current writing process. The space gives a more immersive and layered experience that supports users to explore and create in a way that is closer to real-life creative habits.
Initial Testing Plan
At the current stage, I plan to use low-fidelity prototypes to present the core interaction methods of the XR project and conduct some initial user testing. I will try to use these interactions to verify the basic interface layout, function settings, and the connection and match with user understanding. For target user selection, I prefer to choose students who have the habit of writing or often use word processing software (such as Word) as potential user groups. I will encourage participants to speak out their thinking process and logic chain through the think-aloud protocol, and observe whether users can naturally understand the functions. 

Based on this, I will mainly record: 1. interaction points that are easily misunderstood, 2. interaction points that cause confusion or visual barriers, 3. users’ functional feedback. I will then do iteration design based on this, and gradually improve the project in the medium-fidelity and final stage to create a more immersive interaction experience.

Testing plan for interactive prototype 

Testing Objective
From my concept above, I have identified the assumption that users can easily understand the interaction flow of placing sticky notes, typing on the paper, and archiving documents in the XR writing space.
This test aims to discover whether users can naturally understand these actions and if the space provides a smooth and immersive writing experience.

Testing  Methodologies
This testing plan will use the think-aloud method and observational testing to evaluate a digital prototype made in Unity. Participants will be asked to complete a few tasks while speaking their thoughts out loud, and I will observe how they interact with the interface.

Prototype description/requirments
The prototype was designed to provide interactivity that allows me to test the basic concept of XR writing.
It includes:
The ability to drag and type on a virtual sticky note
Accessing a virtual document paper
Dragging paper into an archive shelf
The space is made with simple visuals and shows how users move and interact with elements in an XR writing environment.

Data collection method

During the testing process, I will:
Ask participants to complete 3 short tasks (stick a note, write on paper, archive document)
Use think-aloud to record their thoughts and reactions
Take observation notes about their facial expression, hesitation, and body movement
Do a short post-test interview to collect feedback on confusion points or suggestions

Testing Setup
To prepare for the test, I need to make sure my prototype is working in Unity and can be opened on the computer.
I will prepare the scene and interactions that I want users to try, like dragging sticky notes or typing on papers.
I will also print or write down some simple instructions so users can follow the task.
Before users come, I will open the Unity scene and get the screen ready for them.
I will also prepare a notebook or Google Doc to write down what they say or do during the test.

Testing process: (also considering the schedule/time)
1.Explain the project to the participant. (1 min)
2.Guide the tester to enter the testing environment and explain the main interaction types. (1 min)
3.Invite the tester to use the prototype and use the “think aloud” method during the process. (3-5 min)
4.Observe and record the tester’s natural behavior, understanding of the interaction, and how smooth the operation is. (3-5 min)
5. Do a short interview to collect useful feedback. (2-3 min)
